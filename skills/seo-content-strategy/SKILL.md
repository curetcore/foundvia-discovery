# Content grounded in customer demand

Inspect the product, intended customer, existing pages, support questions, and authorized search data. Distinguish supplied evidence from assumptions. If demand data is missing, propose a small experiment instead of claiming a keyword volume.

## Choose a useful page

Define the reader's problem and what they should be able to do afterward. Prefer improving a relevant existing page when it already answers the same question. Differentiate a tutorial, product explanation, comparison, and documentation by purpose, not a publishing quota.

Use the [brief template](references/content-brief-template.md) to specify the question, original proof, direct answer, supporting sections, relevant links, next action, owner, and measurement window. Include actual examples, tested steps, limitations, and checked source dates. Length follows the information needed; word counts are not ranking requirements.

## Keep publication truthful

Use real publication and significant-update records. Shared dates are valid. Do not reset dates on every build or invent a percentage-change threshold. Do not publish fabricated testimonials, pricing, usage figures, or author credentials. Explain AI assistance when material to the reader; review generated claims before publication.

Schema must describe visible content. FAQ sections can help readers, but FAQ markup is not a discovery prerequisite and Google's FAQ rich-result feature has been removed. Serialize JSON-LD safely in implementations.

## Measure the experiment

Define one page, its reader question, conversion event, and a follow-up date. Keep impressions, clicks, sessions, activation, and revenue separate. Evaluate whether the resource helped rather than attributing every change to one edit. Deliver the brief and proposed draft within the requested scope; posting and deployment require authorization.
